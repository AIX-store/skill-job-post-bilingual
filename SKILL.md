# Bilingual Job Post

A job post is a filter: it should attract people who can do the job and help everyone else self-select out. Be concrete about the work, honest about the package, and careful with requirements that exclude without reason.

## When to use
- A hiring manager has a role and rough notes, and needs a posting.
- An existing ad brings many unqualified or too few applicants.
- The role must be advertised in both English and Arabic.

## Inputs to gather (ask if missing)
1. Job title as candidates would search for it, plus the internal level.
2. Team, manager's role, and why the role exists now (growth, backfill, new product).
3. What success looks like after 3, 6 and 12 months.
4. Location, on-site / hybrid / remote, travel, working week and hours.
5. Salary range and key benefits, or whether the company chooses not to publish them.
6. Hard requirements (legally or practically necessary) and preferences.
7. Hiring process steps and timeline.

## Method
1. **Title.** Use the market's common title; avoid "ninja", "rockstar", "guru". Put seniority in the title if it matters.
2. **Summary (3-4 sentences).** What the team does, what this person will own, and who they work with.
3. **Outcomes.** Three to five results expected in the first year. These attract stronger candidates than a duty list.
4. **Responsibilities.** Five to eight bullets, each starting with a verb, ordered by time spent.
5. **Requirements, split honestly.**
   - *Must have*: at most five; each one you would actually reject a candidate for lacking.
   - *Nice to have*: skills you can teach.
   - Replace years-of-experience with demonstrated ability where possible ("has shipped and maintained a production API" vs "5+ years").
   - Degrees only when truly required for the job or licensing.
6. **Package.** Salary range, payment frequency, and benefits that matter in the market: medical insurance (and family cover), annual leave days, housing or transport allowance, annual flight tickets, end-of-service benefits, training budget, visa sponsorship, relocation. If the client will not publish salary, add a line telling candidates when it will be shared.
7. **Working-week norms.** State the working days and hours explicitly. For example, many Gulf employers work Sunday-Thursday while others work Monday-Friday; Egyptian employers commonly use Sunday-Thursday with Friday-Saturday weekends. Mention reduced Ramadan hours if the company applies them, and the remote or hybrid policy in days per week.
8. **How to apply.** Steps, expected timeline, what to send, and a contact for accessibility adjustments.
9. **Arabic version.** Write natively in Modern Standard Arabic. Choose the Arabic title candidates search for (often both Arabic and English in parentheses). Address the reader inclusively, for example using forms such as "المرشح/المرشحة" sparingly or neutral phrasing like "ستتولى في هذا الدور" addressed directly, and avoid assuming gender.

## Inclusive wording checklist
- No age limits, nationality, gender, marital status, religion or appearance requirements unless a specific law requires them - and if a local rule applies (for example, roles reserved for nationals under workforce localisation programmes), state it neutrally and have HR or a legal adviser confirm the wording.
- Avoid coded words ("young, dynamic team", "digital native", "aggressive").
- Language requirements stated as levels ("professional working Arabic and English").
- Physical requirements only if essential, with the reason.
- An equal-opportunity line the company actually stands behind.

## Compliance note
Employment rules on advertising, localisation quotas, visas and benefits differ by country and change often. Treat this skill's output as a draft; confirm final wording with your HR team, the relevant labour authority or an employment adviser before publishing.

## Output format
```
EN
Title / Location / Work model / Working week
About the team
What you'll achieve in your first year
What you'll do
Must have
Nice to have
What we offer (salary range, benefits)
How to apply

AR
(same sections, written natively)

OPEN ITEMS FOR THE HIRING MANAGER
- e.g. salary range not provided
```

## Worked example (fragment)
Notes: "Need a senior backend dev, Node, Riyadh, hybrid, 5+ yrs, degree, team of 6 building payments."
- Must have: "Has designed and run production services handling payments or other money movement."
- Nice to have: "Experience with the local payment rails and card schemes."
- Removed: degree requirement (not essential; flagged in open items for confirmation).
- Working week: "Sunday to Thursday, hybrid: three office days in Riyadh."
- AR title: "مهندس/ة برمجيات أول - الأنظمة الخلفية (Senior Backend Engineer)".

## Pitfalls
- A must-have list of twelve items that no real candidate meets.
- Copying the internal job grade description verbatim.
- Hiding the salary and the work model, then losing candidates late in the process.
- Arabic that reads like a translation of English idioms.
